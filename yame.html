Yame, the town

One city, six districts

Yame took in Joyo Town in 2006, then Kurogi, Tachibana, Yabe and Hoshino in 2010. From the old merchant town of Yame-Fukushima on the plain to the source of the Yabe River in the mountains, altitude, work and daily life all change. Many people feel pride in their own district first.

More than a tea town

Next to Yame tea (first in Japan for gyokuro by production value), the town makes Yame lanterns (first in Japan by volume), Yame-Fukushima Buddhist altars, Yame washi paper, stone lanterns, cedar timber, Onokoyaki pottery, sake and fruit. From outside, people often see only "Yame = tea".

The matcha crisis: three structural problems
  1. The gains do not reach farmers. Wholesalers sit in between, so many tea farmers sell at the old price while world prices rise.
  2. Gyokuro or matcha. Moving to matcha makes economic sense, but the gyokuro tradition could be lost.
  3. Matcha no longer means Japan. Chinese production leads the world market, and matcha lattes of unknown quality sell for more than coffee.
Yame 2040 Vision

Yame City is drafting a vision for 2040 together with its residents, through dialogue sessions that began in July 2026. Residents have named what they want to keep. The next question is how to keep it.

Sources: Yame City Population Vision (revised March 2021), Yame City census page, Yame 2040 Vision website. The 2040 figure is a projection from 2021. Night photo: Crossroad Fukuoka.

City and countryside, one hour apart

Fukuoka is one of the fastest growing cities in Japan. One hour south, Yame is a city of 60,000 spread over mountains, tea fields and an old merchant town. Moving between the two is part of what we want you to feel: how close the city is, and how different daily life becomes.

WHY WE ARE HERE

Our mission in Yame

MISSION
Learn with the people of Yame, and offer an outside view they can use to decide what to keep and how.
How we work here
  • We are not here as tourists. This program, an official pilot of the Japan Tourism Agency, exists to bring new economic and social possibilities to the region.
  • No product is expected. What matters is dialogue with the people of Yame and the ideas that come out of it.
  • Ask about the problems people live with, not only the craft. Listen before you suggest.
  • Leave relationships and ideas that continue after Oct 14.

Why we see six worlds

Each day the group splits in two and goes to different places. Food, forest and water, color, tea, thread and fermentation, seen side by side, show how these trades grow from the same mountains and the same water.

SUZUPAY

Paying with SuzuPay

SuzuPay is a QR payment service you use in your phone browser, with no app to install. Points are in yen: 1 pt = ¥1. In this program it is how we leave a real trace in the local economy.

1Create an account

Open main.suzupay.com/login and sign up as "Customer" with your email and a password.

2Verify your email

Open the link in the email, then enter a display name and phone number.

3Enter your coupon

Staff will give you a code that starts with SZP-. It adds 5,000 pts after your email is verified. You can enter it at sign-up or later in Settings.

4Pay by QR

Open "QR Payment", enter the amount and show your QR to the staff. Or scan the shop's QR and confirm the shop name.

Good to know
  • Your QR expires after about 2 minutes. Tap "Refresh code" for a new one.
  • Points are valid for 6 months.
  • No refunds after payment. Check the amount first.
  • Switch between English and Japanese at the top right of the screen.
  • SuzuPay does not work at Unagi no Nedoko.

Satoyama Living: Everyday Lessons

Former Ouchi Residence / Odotani no Sato · Tachibana
WhenOct 11, 13:00 to 16:30
CapacityMax 6
Getting thereBus
MealSatoyama lunch included (has meat dishes)
About the place

A half day in the hills of Tachibana: a satoyama lunch cooked by local mothers, then konnyaku made the old way with wood ash. Lunch is at the Former Ouchi Residence, birthplace of Ouchi Chozo, a politician who worked for friendship between Japan and China from the Meiji to the early Showa era. A local preservation group restored the house and reopened it in April 2023.

What to see
  • A satoyama lunch made from local seasonal food
  • Making konnyaku with Nakashima-san of Odotani no Sato, set with wood ash
  • The Former Ouchi Residence and the story of saving it
Why it matters

Know-how around pickles, konnyaku, bamboo craft and mountain work is not reaching the next generation. Here, cooking for guests is a way to keep that culture alive.

QUESTION TO CARRYHow can a skill that is only used for guests come back into daily life?
DAY 1 · OPTION 2

Baba Suishaba

Waterwheel incense mill · Joyo
WhenOct 11, leave 14:00, visit 14:30 to 15:30
Getting thereYame City microbus (about 30 min)
Meal13:00 team lunch in town
About the place

A working waterwheel mill that makes incense only from Yame cedar leaves and tabu leaves. A 5.5 m waterwheel drives 15 pestles that grind dried cedar leaves into powder over about 23 hours. Built in 1918 with money from 21 locals, bought by the Baba family in 1961, and rebuilt in 2008 by a father and son team of waterwheel carpenters from Kurume.

What to see
  • Pestles moved only by water, and cedar leaves turning to powder
  • Cedar incense with no added scent or color
  • Baba-san calls himself "the forest's cleaner"
Why it matters

About 60% of Yame is forest. At its peak the area had over 40 incense waterwheels, cut down by imports, electric power and a lack of successors.

QUESTION TO CARRYWhat would a business look like if it started from what the forest leaves behind?

DAY 1 · OPTION 2

Matsunobu Kogei

Wooden tub maker
WhenOct 11, 16:00 to 16:45
Getting there30 min from the mill, 10 min back to town
About the place

A tub maker founded in 1959 and now in its third generation. Rice tubs, sushi tubs, bath pails and pickling barrels, all made by hand from wood and bamboo. The Chikugo area once had around 60 tub makers; only a few remain in Kyushu today.

What to see
  • Hoops woven from Yame bamboo instead of metal
  • Boards of different widths matched by feel (about ten years to learn)
  • Ongoing demand from breweries, temples and taiko drum makers
Why it matters

Yame grows both cedar and bamboo, so the craft kept local work circulating. Some sake breweries are going back to wooden tubs.

QUESTION TO CARRYHow could being close to your raw materials become a strength again?
DAY 2 · OPTION 3

Aizome Kasuri Kobo

Kurume Kasuri indigo workshop · Hirokawa
WhenOct 12, leave 9:30, visit 10:00 to 11:30
CapacityMax 10
Lunch12:00 in town, on your own
About the place

A Kurume Kasuri workshop founded in 1891. It keeps the traditional way of preparing indigo and dyes yarn more than 50 times to build its shades of blue. The fifth generation, Kensuke Yamamura, dyes; the fourth generation weaves.

What to see
  • Indigo made ready by fermentation, called aidate
  • Lye from local oak ash, wheat bran and shell lime from the Ariake Sea
  • Cloth coming out of the vat brown and turning blue in the air
Why it matters

Indigo is alive and needs care every day. Many trades in Yame depend on this kind of upkeep that visitors never see.

QUESTION TO CARRYWhat else in Yame relies on daily care that visitors never see?

Chiyonoen

Organic tea farm · Yabe
WhenOct 12, leave 8:45 (about 60 min), back 13:30
CapacityMax 10
LunchChiyonoen tea house, self-paid
About the place

An organic tea farm at 400 to 600 m in Yabe Village. Snow covers the fields in winter and new buds come late; the farm calls this "late-budding tea from the snowy mountain". It makes green tea, Japanese black tea and oolong, with JAS organic certification since 2015.

What to see
  • Mountain tea fields and the tea factory
  • Lunch at the tea house
  • Fukuoka Design Award grand prize (2024); platinum at the Japanese Tea Award for its oolong (2023)
Why it matters

A chance to hear the matcha crisis from a grower. Yabe residents say they want to grow from 800 people to 2,000.

QUESTION TO CARRYHow do you tell a story about "late" and "small" in a matcha market that rewards fast and big?
DAY 2 · EVERYONE

Yame-Fukushima craft walk

Old town makers and merchant houses
WhenOct 12, meet 13:30, until 17:15
Meeting pointUnagi no Nedoko, former Terasaki residence (327 Honmachi)
GuidesRei Watanabe and Hirai-san
About the place

A walk through the white-walled old town. Ogata Butsudan does the final stage of Yame-Fukushima altars, lacquering, gilding and assembling. Ito Gonjiro Shoten makes Yame lanterns, first in Japan by volume. The Former Yame District Office was built in the Meiji era and restored by an NPO; part of it is Asahiya Saketen. Konomi Honke is Kyushu's oldest tea wholesaler, founded in the early 1700s, and gave "Yame tea" its name.

What to see
  • 13:30 Souvenir shopping at Unagi no Nedoko
  • 15:00 Chakabuki (a tea tasting game) and matcha with Imamura-san
  • 16:30 Re:crafted: a showcase of Yame crafts
  • 17:00 Konomi Honke, a late Edo town house and its tea roasting room
Why it matters

The white-walled streets and craft skills are what residents most want to keep. Here we meet the people who keep the town working.

QUESTION TO CARRYWhat would it take to turn the old streets from a place to look at back into a place to work?

Shimogawa Orimono

Kurume Kasuri weaver · open factory · Tsue
WhenOct 13, leave 9:15, visit 9:30 to 11:30
CapacityAbout 7
Getting thereYame City microbus (about 10 min)
About the place

Founded in 1948, the last Kurume Kasuri weaver in Yame City. Third generation Kyozo Shimogawa took over at 22 after university, and his son has now joined. The workshop often works with artists from Finland, Sweden and other countries.

What to see
  • 20 looms driven by belts from one motor in the ceiling
  • A talk by Kyozo-san on the future of Kurume Kasuri
  • The gallery, with fabric sold by the meter
Why it matters

He keeps the old way while trying new sales channels and overseas projects. He also plans to join the Day 3 share-back.

QUESTION TO CARRYCould opening a factory to visitors work for other makers in Yame?
DAY 3 · OPTION 6

Takahashi Shoten (Shigemasu)

Sake brewery · in the old town
WhenOct 13, leave 9:15 on foot, visit 9:30 to 11:30
CapacityAbout 7
ProgramTour, tasting and talk
About the place

A sake brewery founded in Yame in 1717, now led by its 19th generation and known for the Shigemasu brand. It uses water from the underground flow of the Yabe River and Fukuoka rice, including Gin no Sato sake rice grown in Yame.

What to see
  • The process from rice polishing to pressing
  • Koji trays tracked with QR codes next to traditional methods

A closed school, reopened for business

The former Kawasaki Elementary School is being turned into a base for people who want to start businesses in Yame, with a shared office for entrepreneurs. It is our workspace for Day 3, and the place where we give our ideas back to Yame.

The Yame Local Venture School

Launched in June 2026, the school grows "seeds of business built on local resources, culture and tradition". Its idea is simple: Yame is rich in tea, food and crafts, but their value does not travel far enough. More people running real businesses, not volunteering, is how the town meets population decline.

  • A business contest and venture school: 8 teams selected, with mentoring, lectures and a final pitch in February 2027
  • Kickoff on Oct 17, 2026, a few days after our visit
  • Judges include Mayor Yutaro Minohara and Takahiro Shiramizu, founder of Unagi no Nedoko
  • Discounted desks in the school's shared office for selected teams
